# Judging self-assessment: Repo City

Meta VR Start Developer Competition 2026. Four criteria, 25% each. Evidence is linked; gaps are marked honestly.

## Entry choices
- **Track:** Productivity. **Division:** New Experience.
- **Special awards we aim at:** Boldest Original Concept; Best Agentic Interaction (the guide), with the caveat below that it is offline and scripted from real data, not a live model.
- **Platform:** WebXR in Meta Quest Browser, IWSDK 1.1.0, `immersive-ar` (passthrough), hands only, seated.

## Criterion 1: Innovation & Creativity
- **The new idea:** a *live test result* as something physical you can touch. A failing test is a building on fire on your real desk; you lift its roof, touch the smouldering floor (the function with the bug), read the failing check, and watch the fire go out when the real suite passes. Code cities exist (CodeCharta, BabiaXR, ExplorViz); none are hands-first in mixed reality and none turn test failures into something you poke. No Meta Store equivalent found (`research/idea-ranking.md`).
- **Prior art:** code cities go back to CodeCity and CodeCharta. Claude Clan (2026) draws a 2D isometric city on a desktop screen. Repo City is different: it sits in mixed reality on your real desk, it's hands only, failing tests are fires you touch, and buildings open into their functions as floors.
- **Real data, not a mock:** the bundled city is semver 7.7.3 (npm's version library, ISC) with a **real bug from its history put back**: upstream commit 2471d75 (Sep 2025) added one line to `parseComparator`; without it, 20 real assertions fail with `TypeError: Invalid comparator: >2.x+build`. The fire panel shows that error and its real stack; the fix panel shows the real upstream diff and commit, and the crane installs it. Previously (before 18 Nov evening) the bug was one we wrote, which read as staged; we replaced it. We ran the **genuine** test suite (8,827 assertions; 3 fail) and the fixed one (all pass), and baked both runs into the city.
- **A second insight from the same city:** git history (commits per file) times complexity gives a hotspot score; the top ~15% carry scaffolding. Tests show where the code is broken now; scaffolding shows where it keeps changing.
- **The app reads itself:** the second city is Repo City's own source, generated by the same pipeline.
- **Supporting evidence that this helps:** a 24-person study found faster answers in a VR code city at equal accuracy (doi 10.1109/VISSOFT52517.2021.00011).

## Criterion 2: Experience Design
- **Made for the seat you're in:** the panel, plaques and the opened building turn to face whoever sits at the table now (the headset passed to a judge in another chair, or a recenter: `XRReferenceSpace` `reset`). The e2e suite moves the head to another side of the table and finishes the journey from there.
- **From desk to horizon:** "Street level" grows the city past the table to a hazy horizon (the only mode that hides passthrough), with one-hand pinch-drag to move through it and two-hand pinch to scale.
- **The pitch:** you're vibecoding, waiting for your agent; you put on the headset and your own repo is a city on your desk, with your real failing test on fire (`npm run city:repo`).
- **One verb in two seconds:** pinch a roof and lift it. Secondary verbs: touch a floor, poke a plaque, two-hand pinch to scale/rotate.
- **A place to start:** the guide first names the town hall (the package's entry file, a domed civic building), then flies to the fire.
- **The fix is physical:** on *Run the tests* a tower crane lifts the smouldering floor out on its hook (still burning, until steam) and lowers in the fixed one with a clunk and a puff of dust, then the green wave runs.
- **Awe moment within ~60 s:** the city grows out of your table with a dust ring; one tower is burning; you lift its roof and it slides out onto the desk beside the city as a stack of lit floors, one smouldering.
- **Teach in context, with a guide:** the lantern guide speaks (browser TTS) and captions every step on the panel, flies over to point at the burning roof and then at the smouldering floor, and repeats the step after 15 s of no progress (Meta's "contextual reminders"). Every step also has a plaque on the table ("Open the fire", "Find the bug") so no one gets stuck.
- **Intent guardrails** (Meta's hands guidance):
  - a hand passing over the flames does nothing; a fingertip held in them for 0.3 s opens the building;
  - a floor stack growing around a resting finger never selects a floor (the finger must be seen outside first);
  - a pinch that lifts less than a quarter and isn't a tap puts the roof back;
  - tracking lost mid-lift keeps what you had;
  - buttons debounce per id and globally, so a new plaque appearing under a finger can't fire.
- **Feel at the point of contact:** hover glow + tick on buildings, floors and plaques; a label over the hovered building; sound on lift, close, floor, alarm, test ticks, steam and the success chord (all synthesised).
- **Either hand:** the building comes out on the side of the hand that took it; the e2e suite runs right- and left-handed.
- **Inclusive defaults:** captions for all speech; sitting only; no time pressure; nothing punishes waiting.

## Criterion 3: Technical Implementation
- **Pipeline as code** (`tools/repo-to-city`, Node, no new dependencies): TypeScript compiler API parses JS/TS into functions with spans and a cyclomatic-complexity proxy; an import graph from the syntax tree (require, import, export-from, dynamic import); a deterministic treemap that maximises the smallest building (pinchability); a tap-compatible shim that runs semver's real tests per file; summaries for the offline guide. `npm run city` regenerates everything, including the baked light; 50 unit tests (`npm run test:unit`) cover layout (including the affinity gate), parser, entry-point resolution, git-history parsing and the hotspot score, the city contract, the light bake and placement. Git history: `--numstat` parsed with renames, semver's from a blobless clone up to its tag (cached in fixtures); an optional affinity layout puts coupled districts side by side only when that cuts the coupling cost by ≥10% (both shipped cities fall back to size: scores in `stats.layout`).
- **Rendering on a mobile GPU:** instanced buildings with a procedural window shader (windows sized in metres from the instance scale, lit/unlit per window, floor slabs, contact darkening at the foot of walls, burning rows and a charred roof driven by per-instance attributes); exploded floors reuse the same material; CPU particle fire in two draws (teardrop flame sprites, embers, smoke, steam); lit import routes as one ribbon mesh with a flowing-dash shader, plus data-flow dots placed entirely on the GPU (one `Points` draw; attributes rebuilt only on open/close); a fix crane whose cable and hook move in the vertex shader (one draw), with the failing floor's own instance as the moving slab; scaffolding as one instanced draw. The five city-life features add +2 draw calls on Quest when idle (+3 during the crane's 2.4 s).
- **Lighting baked at build time** (`tools/repo-to-city/light-bake.mjs`):
  - a CPU ray tracer over the city's boxes (buildings, rooftop plant and trees, from the same generator the app draws with) writes ambient occlusion and soft sun visibility into a 512² atlas: a top-down map of the ground plus a 16² tile for every wall and roof;
  - the atlas ships as KTX2 (UASTC, 5 box-filtered mips so tiles never bleed), and the Basis transcoder is served from our own origin;
  - the shaders apply AO to indirect light and sun visibility to direct light, after three.js has accumulated them;
  - small props carry per-vertex AO;
  - reflections come from a 128-px PMREM of a desk room whose window sits where the key light is: windows are low-roughness glass, and each district's roofs have their own roughness.
- **Room understanding:** plane scoring with semantic labels in both vocabularies, round/small-table handling, a placement report line, `XRAnchor` once placed.
- **Testing without a headset:** `npm run test:xr` drives the whole hands-only journey in IWER (18 checks; right hand with 3 mm jitter; left hand with 5 mm jitter and pinch-drag), from structured dev logs with page/seq pinning.

## Decisions (asked of every project)

### Physics: no rigid-body engine
**Decision:** no Havok / IWSDK physics. Buildings are instanced static geometry; motion is eased springs and tweens (the city rising with a back-ease overshoot, the roof and floors opening, the building sliding out, the selected floor drawer, particles).
**Why:** nothing in the mechanic is about mass, collision or stacking. A physics engine would add a WASM module, per-frame cost and failure modes (buildings jittering on a moving anchor) without serving the verb "lift the roof and read". Controlled animation also keeps the layout readable and the e2e tests deterministic.

### Lighting: baked per city, no runtime shadows
**Decision:** bake ambient occlusion and soft sun shadows at build time (one KTX2 texture per city, ~90 KB) and remove the runtime shadow map.
**Why:**
- The city never changes shape except the one building you lift out, so live shadows were re-rendering the whole city every frame something moved (rise, open, drag, sweep) for no extra information.
- Baking gives what a shadow map can't on a mobile GPU: soft penumbrae and real ambient occlusion between neighbours.
- It saves ~8 MB of shadow targets and a render pass.
- The key light sits to the side and about 46° up, so the shadows fall where a seated player looks, across roofs and district plates, not behind buildings.
- A city made with `tools/repo-to-city` gets its bake in the same step: KTX2 with KTX-Software installed, else PNG.

### Engine: WebXR + IWSDK, not Unity, Unreal or Godot
**Decision:** stay on WebXR/IWSDK.
**Why:** Godot 4.7 became available mid-day, Unity/Unreal were not installed. A native build has no equivalent of our IWER end-to-end harness, so every hands-only check would depend on the one shared headset, and it would need a "Competition" release channel set up before the deadline. With ~12 hours left, the WebXR path (already working end to end, testable in an emulator, shipped as a URL) was the lower-risk route to a polished entry.

### AI guide: offline by design
**Decision:** the guide's words are precomputed from the real repo at build time (`tools/repo-to-city/semver-summaries.json`) and spoken with the browser's speech synthesis. No API key, no server.
**Why:** the "take it away" rule and reliability for judges. A live LLM endpoint (a Vercel function) is the obvious next step, but it needs a paid key and the user's consent, and the experience must stand without it.

## Beyond the team's skills (what we adopted and why)
- **Canvas2D everywhere instead of UIKit panels:** code needs a monospace font, syntax colours, line highlights and fit-to-card text; Canvas gives full control in one texture per card and avoids UIKit's emulator poke issues. Buttons are our own poke/pinch-tested plaques.
- **Procedural window shader via `onBeforeCompile`** on `MeshStandardMaterial`: keeps PBR lighting while adding per-instance windows, fire and the baked-light lookup, in one draw call.
- **Baked light for generated geometry:** a city has no fixed mesh to bake in Blender, so the bake is a small ray tracer over the layout's boxes, run by the same pipeline that makes the city. The atlas is indexed per instance and per face.
- **Panel draw order fix:** transparent panels without depth writes paint over transparent effects drawn earlier, so the panel draws first (`renderOrder = 1`). This hid the whole fire until bisected with a live probe (`tools/probe.mjs`).

## Gaps (honest)
- Verified on a Quest 3 (18 Nov evening, Quest Browser 156): hands detected, a steady 72 fps (p95 14.6 ms) at ~34 draw calls, the whole journey. Re-measured after the fixes: street level holds 72 fps; the hitch when the city appears dropped from 818 ms to 122–180 ms, and the first building opening from 207 to 100 ms. The test-run dip was still there (67.5 fps), so the live part of that panel moved to a small separate card; not yet re-measured on the device.
- Agent fix proposals as worker characters (the team's idea) are not built: no time before the freeze.
- The guide is scripted from real data, not conversational.
- "Your own repo" is one command on your machine (`npm run city:repo -- <path>`, Node's built-in test runner) and a `?city=` link; there is no in-headset file picker, and other test runners need their results passed in.
