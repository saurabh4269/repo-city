# Judging self-assessment: Repo City

Meta VR Start Developer Competition 2026. Four criteria, 25% each. Evidence is linked; gaps are marked honestly.

## Entry choices
- **Track:** Productivity. **Division:** New Experience.
- **Special awards we aim at:** Boldest Original Concept; Best Agentic Interaction (the guide), with the caveat below that it is offline and scripted from real data, not a live model.
- **Platform:** WebXR in Meta Quest Browser, IWSDK 1.1.0, `immersive-ar` (passthrough), hands only, seated.

## Criterion 1: Innovation & Creativity
- **The new idea:** a *live test result* as something physical you can touch. A failing test is a building on fire on your real desk; you lift its roof, touch the smouldering floor (the function with the bug), read the failing check, and watch the fire go out when the real suite passes. Code cities exist (CodeCharta, BabiaXR, ExplorViz); none are hands-first in mixed reality and none turn test failures into something you poke. No Meta Store equivalent found (`research/idea-ranking.md`).
- **Real data, not a mock:** the bundled city is semver 7.7.3 (npm's version library, ISC). We introduced one realistic one-line bug (swapped operators in `ranges/subset.js` `higherGT`), ran the **genuine** test suite (8,827 assertions; 3 fail) and the fixed one (all pass), and baked both runs into the city.
- **The app reads itself:** the second city is Repo City's own source, generated by the same pipeline.
- **Supporting evidence that this helps:** a 24-person study found faster answers in a VR code city at equal accuracy (doi 10.1109/VISSOFT52517.2021.00011).

## Criterion 2: Experience Design
- **One verb in two seconds:** pinch a roof and lift it. Secondary verbs: touch a floor, poke a plaque, two-hand pinch to scale/rotate.
- **Awe moment within ~60 s:** the city grows out of your table with a dust ring; one tower is burning; you lift its roof and it slides out onto the desk beside the city as a stack of lit floors, one smouldering.
- **Teach in context, with a guide:** the lantern guide speaks (browser TTS) and captions every step on the panel, flies over to point at the burning roof and then at the smouldering floor, and repeats the step after 15 s of no progress (Meta's "contextual reminders"). Every step also has a plaque on the table ("Open the fire", "Find the bug") so no one gets stuck.
- **Intent guardrails** (Meta's hands guidance):
  - a hand passing over the flames does nothing; a fingertip held in them for 0.3 s opens the building;
  - a floor stack growing around a resting finger never selects a floor (the finger must be seen outside first);
  - a pinch that lifts less than a quarter and isn't a tap puts the roof back;
  - tracking lost mid-lift keeps what you had;
  - buttons debounce per id and globally, so a new plaque appearing under a finger can't fire.
- **Feel at the point of contact:** hover glow + tick on buildings, floors and plaques; a label over the hovered building; sound on lift, close, floor, alarm, test ticks, steam and the success chord (all synthesised).
- **Either hand:** the building comes out on the side of the hand that took it; the e2e suite runs right- and left-handed.
- **Inclusive defaults:** captions for all speech; sitting only; no time pressure; nothing punishes waiting.

## Criterion 3: Technical Implementation
- **Pipeline as code** (`tools/repo-to-city`, Node, no new dependencies): TypeScript compiler API parses JS/TS into functions with spans and a cyclomatic-complexity proxy; an import graph from the syntax tree (require, import, export-from, dynamic import); a deterministic treemap that maximises the smallest building (pinchability); a tap-compatible shim that runs semver's real tests per file; summaries for the offline guide. `npm run city` regenerates everything; 28 unit tests (`npm run test:unit`) cover layout, parser, the city contract and placement.
- **Rendering on a mobile GPU:** instanced buildings with a procedural window shader (windows sized in metres from the instance scale, lit/unlit per window, floor slabs, contact darkening at the foot of walls, burning rows and a charred roof driven by per-instance attributes); exploded floors reuse the same material; CPU particle fire in two draws (teardrop flame sprites, embers, smoke, steam); lit import routes as one ribbon mesh with a flowing-dash shader; one shadow map that updates only on change.
- **Room understanding:** plane scoring with semantic labels in both vocabularies, round/small-table handling, a placement report line, `XRAnchor` once placed.
- **Testing without a headset:** `npm run test:xr` drives the whole hands-only journey in IWER (13 checks; right hand with 3 mm jitter; left hand with 5 mm jitter and pinch-drag), from structured dev logs with page/seq pinning.

## Decisions (asked of every project)

### Physics: no rigid-body engine
**Decision:** no Havok / IWSDK physics. Buildings are instanced static geometry; motion is eased springs and tweens (the city rising with a back-ease overshoot, the roof and floors opening, the building sliding out, the selected floor drawer, particles).
**Why:** nothing in the mechanic is about mass, collision or stacking. A physics engine would add a WASM module, per-frame cost and failure modes (buildings jittering on a moving anchor) without serving the verb "lift the roof and read". Controlled animation also keeps the layout readable and the e2e tests deterministic.

### Engine: WebXR + IWSDK, not Unity, Unreal or Godot
**Decision:** stay on WebXR/IWSDK.
**Why:** Godot 4.7 became available mid-day, Unity/Unreal were not installed. A native build has no equivalent of our IWER end-to-end harness, so every hands-only check would depend on the one shared headset, and it would need a "Competition" release channel set up before the deadline. With ~12 hours left, the WebXR path (already working end to end, testable in an emulator, shipped as a URL) was the lower-risk route to a polished entry.

### AI guide: offline by design
**Decision:** the guide's words are precomputed from the real repo at build time (`tools/repo-to-city/semver-summaries.json`) and spoken with the browser's speech synthesis. No API key, no server.
**Why:** the "take it away" rule and reliability for judges. A live LLM endpoint (a Vercel function) is the obvious next step, but it needs a paid key and the user's consent, and the experience must stand without it.

## Beyond the team's skills (what we adopted and why)
- **Canvas2D everywhere instead of UIKit panels:** code needs a monospace font, syntax colours, line highlights and fit-to-card text; Canvas gives full control in one texture per card and avoids UIKit's emulator poke issues. Buttons are our own poke/pinch-tested plaques.
- **Procedural window shader via `onBeforeCompile`** on `MeshStandardMaterial`: keeps PBR lighting and shadows while adding per-instance windows and fire, in one draw call.
- **Panel draw order fix:** transparent panels without depth writes paint over transparent effects drawn earlier, so the panel draws first (`renderOrder = 1`). This hid the whole fire until bisected with a live probe (`tools/probe.mjs`).
